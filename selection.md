# Selection — The Funniest Out-of-Office

Judge criteria, in order: (1) a one-line OOO a real person would set and send;
(2) a genuine surprise / twist; (3) a voice of its own, not a template;
(4) fresh, not the thousand-inboxes joke; (5) every word earns its place.

## Elimination

- Round 1 (C4 freshness): cut #2, #11, #12, #1 — the jokes everyone has read.
- Round 2 (C2 twist): cut #6, #7 — twists land soft.
- Round 3 (duel of the top four): #10 vs #8 — #10's sentience-negotiation twist is bigger and fresher than the well-worn therapy frame. #10 vs #5 — #5 is a logic joke; #10 is more visual and more voiced. #10 vs #3 — Gary is human but the twist is milder; #10 wins on C2 and C4.

## Winner

**#10** — `Out of office. The coffee machine finally achieved sentience and I'm negotiating its demands.` (93 chars)

Why it wins: it reads as a line a real person would actually set (C1: a named office object, a plausible absence); the twist is genuine and visual (C2: sentience, then negotiation — two turns, not one); the voice is a specific long-suffering employee, not a template (C3: "finally", "negotiating its demands"); the joke is fresh — no thousand-inboxes version of it exists (C4); and every word is load-bearing (C5: the second "office" was cut after the first draft — "Out of office" already sets the scene, so "the coffee machine" is enough; cut "finally" and the voice goes; cut "negotiating its demands" and the second turn goes). It also fits the sponsor's prize: it works as the house's own OOO reply for a month.
